Horizon Sweeps

A single call of solve() uses one horizon pair H = [H1, H2] (Section ??). A horizon sweep
solves one scenario for several pairs and compares the solved cases. Two functions implement
the sweeps: sweep operating horizons varies H2 for one H1, and sweep horizon grid varies
H1 and H2 on a grid. No input key starts a sweep, and solve() never runs one. Both functions
are exported by the package (Section 3.1).

Cost Measures and the Ranking Criteria

Let x, z, xr be the solution of a case. The cost of day k is

                              F    FL

      ck = CM xi0k +                            CR,i zik + Crep,i xrik ,      (1)

                              i=1  i=1 =1

where zik = 0 for a cell with CR,i = 0 (no variable z is built, Section ??). The cost of the
transitory phase and the cost of the operating phase are

                         H1                  T

      Jinit = ck,                  Jop =          ck ,        J¯op = Jop/H2.  (2)

                         k=1              k=H1+1

The peak damage is u = maxi,k  µik, and the damage penalty is CD u. The result keys
are J initialization, J op, J op average (= J¯op), peak damage and damage penalty (Sec-
tion ??).

A solve with objective mode = "operating average" minimises J¯op + CD u (Section ??). The

transitory phase has no cost in this objective, but it is still constrained. With objective mode =

"evaluation total" and an evaluation horizon E > H1 (days), the solve minimises the projected

cost

                             PE = Jinit + (E - H1) J¯op + CD u.               (3)

PE is the cost of the transitory phase, then of the operating phase repeated until day E,
plus the damage penalty. Two result keys carry it: evaluation objective holds PE, and
projected evaluation cost holds PE - CD u.

A case is feasible when its result holds a solution and its status is not "infeasible", not
"unbounded" and not "inf or unbounded". The MIP gap is the relative gap between the
objective of the best solution found and the best bound (MIP: mixed-integer program). A case
is proven when it is feasible and Gurobi reports the status "optimal" at the MIP gap used for
that case, that is, the run option mip gap of Section ??. A proven case is optimal within that
gap, not exactly optimal. The sweep report records the requested gap of each case, so a reader
sees how strong the proof is.

Ranking criteria. sweep operating horizons ranks cases by J¯op. The loop constraint (Sec-
tion ??) makes the operating phase repeat, so J¯op is the cost per day of the repeating regime. It is
comparable across values of H2, whereas a raw objective over T days is not. sweep horizon grid
ranks by J¯op when no evaluation horizon is given. With an evaluation horizon it ranks by PE,
which also prices the transitory phase, so it is the criterion that compares different values of H1.
In both functions the best proven case is the proven case with the smallest ranking value, and
the best feasible case is the feasible case with the smallest ranking value. A tie goes to the case
evaluated first.

Remark (what the proof certifies). The status "optimal" certifies the objective that the solve
minimised. In the mode operating average this objective is J¯op + CD u, while the ranking
value is J¯op alone. The sweep therefore ranks by the cost per day and uses the damage penalty

only inside each solve.

Early Stop

Let J¯n and H2,n be the cost per day and the operating horizon of the n-th evaluated case. When
cases n - 1 and n are both feasible, the relative gradient of case n is

                         g^n =                   J¯n - J¯n-1           ,  (4)
                                (H2,n - H2,n-1) max{ |J¯n-1|,  mach}

with mach the machine precision of double-precision numbers. Case n is classified "increase" if
g^n > gradient tolerance, "decrease" if g^n < -gradient tolerance, and "flat" otherwise.
If one of the two cases is not feasible, or if n = 1, the class is "unavailable". The gradient of
case n is eligible to stop when both cases n - 1 and n report a finite MIP gap that is at most
maximum mip gap for stopping. The function annotates every case with these values, also when
stop on gradient is false.

With stop on gradient = True the function stops after case n when n  minimum cases, the
gradient of case n is eligible to stop, and one of these holds:

 the class of case n is "increase" (stopping reason "cost increase");

 the class of case n is "flat", and the last flat gradients required cases up to n all
   have the class "flat" and an eligible gradient (stopping reason "flat gradient").

The last candidate in operating horizons is always a hard limit. A case solved with the default
mip gap of 0.1 can have a gap above the default value 0.05 of maximum mip gap for stopping.
The sweep records such a case, but it cannot trigger a stop.

with the status "error: " followed by the message and null in every other entry except the
sizes, and continues with the next case. Such a row is neither feasible nor proven. An error does
not stop the sweep, and a warm start never uses the result of a failed case (Section 2).

Warm Starts

A warm start gives Gurobi values for the binary variables, so that the search begins from a
known schedule. A warm start changes the time of a solve, not the model: it does not change
the feasible set or the objective. The package has four mechanisms:

   1. a start from the schedule of a previous result (Gurobi attribute Start), with an optional
       completion;

                                                              5
v1.0 · October 26, 2026                      Fleet Management -- SRS

2. hints from the schedule of a previous result (Gurobi attribute VarHintVal);
3. a relaxation start, which needs no previous result;
4. a greedy heuristic, which constructs a schedule from the input and returns it in the form of

   item 1.

Interface

solve() (Section 3.1) has two keyword arguments for warm starts: warm start (default None)
and warm start mode (default "start"). The argument warm start is a source: a dictionary
with the keys of a result, or the dictionary that the greedy heuristic returns (Section 2.7). A source
must carry the arrays x (shape F  × (M  + 1) × T ) and the integers H1, H2 with T  = H1 + H2 ;
it may carry m and r (shape F  × L × T ). A source without a solution (x is null) is accepted
and has no effect. A result with a solution found before a time limit is a valid source. The sizes
F , M , L may differ from the problem's sizes: the values are set for the indices that both have.

warm start mode is "start", "hint" or "both"; another value raises ValueError. The relax-
ation start is selected by the run options relaxation warm start and relaxation time limit
(Section ??).

Day Mapping

The source has the horizons H1 , H2 and the problem has H1, H2. Day k  {1, . . . , T } of the
problem takes the values of the source day

(k) =  k,                                    k  min{H1, H1 },  (5)
       H1 + 1 + max{0, k - H1 - 1} mod H2 ,  otherwise.

So the transitory days keep their index, and the operating days repeat the operating phase of
the source cyclically. A shorter operating phase can seed a longer one. When H1 > H1 , the days
H1 + 1, . . . , H1 all take the first operating day of the source. When the source has the same sizes
F , M , L, the mapped schedule copies whole days of the source, so it keeps the one-activity rows,

the mission coverage and the depot-day rows of every day. It need not satisfy the dynamics or

the loop rows of the horizons of the problem. For each day k, train i and cell (i, ) the mapped
values are xijk  xij(k) for j = 0, . . . , M , ximk  mi(k) (value 0 if the source has no m), and
xrik  ri(k) when replacement is allowed (allow replacement, Section ??) and the source has
r. Every value is rounded to the nearest integer. Each other binary variable that these values fix

through its defining rows (the selector nik of the indicator form, Eq. (??)) receives that value.

Start, Hints and Completion

Mode "start". The solve sets the attribute Start of every mapped variable (Section 2.2). The
continuous variables get no value. Gurobi tries to complete the start by itself, with limited effort,
and discards a start that it cannot make feasible.

Completion. If the source has the key complete binary start with the value true, the solve
completes the start before it optimises. The time limit of the completion is the source key
completion time limit (seconds, default 60). The steps are:

    1. Copy the model. In the copy, fix every binary variable that received a Start value to that
       value.

    2. Optimise the copy. All binary values are fixed, so the copy has only continuous variables.
    3. If the copy has a solution, set the attribute Start of every variable of the original model

       to the value in the copy. The start is then a complete, feasible point of the original model.

                         6
v1.0 · October 26, 2026                                       Fleet Management -- SRS

   4. Otherwise record the status of the copy. If the copy is infeasible, compute an irreducible
       infeasible subset (IIS) of the copy: a set of rows that is infeasible, and that becomes feasible
       when any one row is removed (Gurobi function computeIIS). Record the names of its linear
       rows and general constraints, at most 50 names.

The solve never relaxes or changes the original model, and it never stops because a completion
failed: the optimisation starts with the binary values only. A completion is requested by the
source, not by an argument of solve(). The greedy heuristic requests it.

Mode "hint". The solve sets the attribute VarHintVal of every mapped variable to its mapped
value, and sets no Start and no completion. Gurobi uses hints to guide its heuristics and its
branching during the whole solve. A hint needs no feasible point, so it also helps when the
mapped schedule does not fit the horizons of the problem and Gurobi discards it as a start. The
hint priority VarHintPri is not set.

Mode "both". The solve sets Start and VarHintVal on the same variables, and performs the
completion if the source requests it.

Relaxation Start

If relaxation warm start is true and no start or hint was applied (the entry applied of
warm start is false), the solve computes a start without a source:

   1. Copy the model, and relax its linear rows with Gurobi's function feasRelaxS, which
       minimises the sum of the absolute violations. The relaxation keeps integrality, variable
       bounds and general constraints.

   2. Optimise the relaxed copy with the time limit max{1, relaxation time limit} seconds
       and the MIP gap 0.05.

   3. If the copy has a solution, set Start of every integer variable of the original model to the
       rounded value of the variable of the same name in the copy.

The relaxed solution is not a feasible point of the original model. It gives integer values only,
and the original model must find its own first solution.

Warm Starts in Sweeps

The sweep functions (Section 1) with warm start=True use, for each case, the result of the

mode warm start mode. A case that raised an error or has no solution is skipped. The first case

Greedy Heuristic

build_greedy_warm_start(cfg , *, repair_trigger =0.70) -> (dict ,
      GreedyWarmStartReport)

The function is in the module fleet management.greedy warm start; the package does not
export it. The argument cfg is the normalised configuration of an input (Section ??). The
trigger repair trigger must lie in [0, 1] (ValueError otherwise). The function works for every
model of this specification. It builds a schedule that tries to satisfy the rows of the model. It
does not try to give a low cost. It returns a source for solve(): the dictionary with the keys H1,
H2, x, m, r, complete binary start (true) and completion time limit (60), and a report.

Construction pass. Keep a proxy state s^i for each cell, with the initial value µ0,i. Treat the
days k = 1, . . . , T in order.

    1. Order the missions by decreasing difficulty maxi, µiinjck/i. A tie goes to the mission with
       the larger index.

    2. Take the missions in this order. Give each one to the train i that has no mission yet on
       day k and minimises the pair max(s^i + µiinjck)/i, (s^i + µiinjck)/i in lexicographic
       order. A tie goes to the smaller index i. Increase s^i by µiinjck for the train and day. The
       input check F > M (Section ??) guarantees a train for every mission.

    3. Let r be the value of repair trigger. A train without a mission stays idle, except when
       some component has s^i > r i. Then the train goes to the depot (xi0k = 1), every such
       component is repaired (xmik = 1), and s^i  (1 - i) s^i for it.

The pass schedules no replacement. The proxy uses the whole-state repair map for every cell,
whatever its repair law.

Loop pass. After the construction pass the missions are fixed, so the cells are independent except
through the depot days of a train. For each cell, compute its reference trajectory  (Section ??)
for the current schedule, with the starting values, the starting anchors and the replacement
values of the inputs. The excess e of the cell is the largest of

     max{0, Tq - Hq 1}/Mq for every tracked quantity and anchor q with natural range Mq (the
       loop row, Section ??);

     the violation of the reliability row of the cell in its exact form, on every day, divided by the
       natural range of the quantity that the row bounds (Sections ??­??).

While e > 10-10, the pass tries every free day k  {H1 + 1, . . . , T }, a day on which the train
has no mission and the cell has no intervention. It puts a repair of the cell on the free day (the
train goes to the depot on that day) and recomputes the excess. It keeps the repair that gives
the smallest excess; a tie goes to the later day. If no repair lowers e by more than 10-10 and
replacement is allowed, the same trial is made with a replacement. If no trial lowers e, the pass
counts one failure for the cell and goes to the next cell. Repairs and replacements of a cell never
use a day on which the train flies a mission.

solve(input_path , results_path="output.yaml", *, warm_start=None ,

solve() reads one input file, builds one Gurobi model for the whole fleet, solves it, and returns
the result dictionary of Section ??. Arrays in the dictionary are NumPy arrays; Gurobi objects
are not stored in it.

   1. The input file must exist (FileNotFoundError) and have the extension .yaml, .yml, .json,
       .h5 or .hdf5 (ValueError).

   2. If results path is not None, the solve resolves it before it reads the input. A path without
       an extension gets the extension .yaml. The extension selects the format of the result file:
       YAML, JSON or HDF5 (as for inputs); another extension raises ValueError. The folder of
       the result file must exist (FileNotFoundError) and be writable (PermissionError). With
       results path=None no file is written.

   3. The input reader reads the file by its extension into a mapping of the keys of Section ??.
       The horizon sweeps and validate use the same reader.

   4. load config normalises the mapping and runs the checks of Section ??. A failed check
       raises ValueError, and no model is built.

   5. The solve builds the model (Section 3.2), applies the warm start (Section 2), optimises,
       extracts the solution, writes the result file if requested, and returns the dictionary.

Every fleet, whatever its models, goes through the same route: no model has a separate solver.
Gurobi errors, such as a missing licence, are not caught.

Validation Tools

The validation tools check a saved result against its input. They do not call the optimiser and
they read no Gurobi object. There are three groups: the structural validator for every model
(Section 4.1), the dashboards (Section 4.2), and the validators of the Gamma model (Section 4.3).
All tools read the input with the input reader of solve() and normalise it with load config
(Section 3.1), and read a result file in the formats that solve() writes. They read the model of
each cell from the result key model.

It checks the safety claim itself, for the saved schedule and without the tracked states. For
each Gamma cell it rebuilds the true damage on each day k = 1, . . . , T as a list of independent
Gamma terms, as in the proof of the class of true states (Section ??): every mission adds a
term with the exact rate of its increment; a repair multiplies the rate of the repairable terms
by 1/(1 - ) (the shape is unchanged); under ARD1 the repaired terms join the frozen part of
the anchor; a replacement starts a new list with the replacement term. It computes the tail
Pr(Dk >  ) of the sum of the terms with the Moschopoulos series. The series writes the law of
a sum of independent Gamma variables with different rates as a mixture of Gamma laws with
non-negative weights. When all rates are equal, the tail is the Gamma tail. The partial sum
of the series is a lower bound of the tail. The validator adds the mixture weight that remains,
which is at most convolution tolerance, and so obtains a certified upper bound of the tail.
If the series does not reach this tolerance within max series terms terms, the validator raises
RuntimeError. The argument convolution tolerance must lie in (0, 1) and max series terms
must be positive. A day passes when the upper bound is at most i + tolerance and the
saved mean is at least the exact mean EDk minus tolerance. Under ARD1 the saved anchor
must also be at least the exact mean of the frozen part, minus tolerance. (The saved values
may be larger: they are upper bounds.) The report adds, to the keys of the replay report,
minimum reliability slack (min( - tail upper bound)), worst reliability (the step that
attains it), maximum remaining mass, the numbers of series terms, and the timing of the tail
computations. With include steps=True the report has the list steps with the terms and
values of every cell and day.
