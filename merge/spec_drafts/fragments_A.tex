\section{Project Overview \& Goals}
\label{sec:overview}
% ================================================================

The Python package \texttt{fleet\_management} schedules a fleet of $F$ trains over a
horizon of $T$ days. Every day, $M$ missions must be flown. Each train flies at most
one mission, spends the day in the depot, or stays idle. Each train has $L$
components, and each component degrades stochastically. On a depot day a component
may be repaired (imperfect repair) or replaced. The schedule is a mixed-integer
program solved with Gurobi.

\textbf{Goal.} Minimise the cost of depot days, repairs and replacements, plus a
penalty on the largest train damage (Section~\ref{sec:objective}), subject to
$\Pr(D_{i\ell k} > \tau_{i\ell}) \leq \varepsilon_{i\ell}$ for every component and
every day, with $D_{i\ell k}$ the degradation and $\tau_{i\ell}$ the failure
threshold. The transitory phase ($H_1$ days) starts from the current state of the
fleet; the operating phase ($H_2$ days) is the schedule to repeat. The requirement
holds on every day of the horizon and of every later repetition of the operating
phase (Section~\ref{sec:loop}).

\textbf{Degradation models} (input \texttt{model}, one per cell):
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Gaussian}: Gaussian random walk; tracks mean and variance
        (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_gaussian}).
  \item \textbf{Inverse Gaussian (IG)}: tracks the mean; cap on the mean
        (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_ig}).
  \item \textbf{Wiener}: Brownian motion with drift; tracks mean and variance; the
        variance grows by $\sigma^2$ per mission day and falls only at a replacement
        (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_wiener}).
  \item \textbf{Gamma}: Gamma increments with mission-dependent rates; tracks the mean;
        cap on the mean (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_gamma}).
  \item \textbf{Rainflow}: distribution-free; the increment law enters through summary
        statistics; one of five tail bounds: Markov, Cantelli, Hoeffding, Bernstein,
        Chernoff (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_rainflow}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Repair and replacement.} Each cell has a repair law
(Section~\ref{sec:repair_laws}): $\ARDinf$ (arithmetic reduction of deterioration with
infinite memory, the default) or $\ARDone$ (arithmetic reduction of deterioration of
order 1). Every model accepts both laws. Each cell may be replaced
(Section~\ref{sec:replacement}). The loop row forces at least one replacement in the
operating phase for a Wiener cell whose train flies a mission in that phase, and for
an $\ARDone$ cell with $\rho_{i\ell} < 1$ that gains mean damage in that phase
(scoping document, section ``Stochastic loop constraint'').

\textbf{Problem class.} A mixed-integer linear program (MILP) when every reliability
row is linear and the damage penalty is \texttt{inf\_norm} or absent ($C_D = 0$);
otherwise a mixed-integer quadratically constrained program (MIQCP). It is nonconvex
when a quadratic reliability row uses the form \texttt{exact}
(Section~\ref{sec:linear_forms}), and convex when the only quadratic row is the
\texttt{quadratic} damage penalty (Section~\ref{sec:damage_reg}).

\textbf{Scope of the package.} One solve (\texttt{solve}); horizon sweeps
(Section~\ref{sec:sweeps}); warm starts (Section~\ref{sec:warm_starts}); the schedule
plot (Section~\ref{sec:plotting}); validation tools (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
Inputs in YAML, JSON or HDF5 (Section~\ref{sec:schema}); outputs in
Section~\ref{sec:output}.

\textbf{Authority.} This specification is authoritative for the formulation and for
the tools. Every change is written here first; the code follows it.

\textbf{Non-goals.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Routing decisions are not optimised: the missions are inputs.
  \item Continuous-time formulations are out of scope: one day is the time unit.
\end{itemize}

$k = 0$ holds constants (initial values and starting anchors).

\section{Objective Function}
\label{sec:objective}
% ================================================================

Cost of day $k$, and costs of the two phases:
\begin{equation}
  c_k = C_M \sum_{i=1}^{F} x_{i0k}
      + \sum_{i=1}^{F}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} C_{R,i\ell}\, z_{i\ell k}
      + \sum_{i=1}^{F}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} C_{\mathrm{rep},i\ell}\, x^r_{i\ell k},
  \label{eq:a_daycost}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{init}} = \sum_{k=1}^{H_1} c_k, \qquad
  J_{\mathrm{op}} = \sum_{k=H_1+1}^{T} c_k .
  \label{eq:a_phases}
\end{equation}
$z_{i\ell k}$ reads $0$ where it is not built; the last sum of $c_k$ is absent when
\texttt{allow\_replacement = false}. The input \texttt{objective\_mode} (default
\texttt{total}) selects the objective:
\begin{equation}
  \min\;
  \begin{cases}
    J_{\mathrm{init}} + J_{\mathrm{op}} + C_D\, u & \texttt{total}, \\[2pt]
    J_{\mathrm{op}} / H_2 + C_D\, u & \texttt{operating\_average}, \\[2pt]
    J_{\mathrm{init}} + \dfrac{E - H_1}{H_2}\, J_{\mathrm{op}} + C_D\, u
      & \texttt{evaluation\_total},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:a_objective}
\end{equation}
with $E$ the input \texttt{evaluation\_horizon}, required in the mode
\texttt{evaluation\_total}, $E > H_1$. The term $C_D\,u$ is present only when
$C_D > 0$.

\textbf{Cost terms} (shapes in Section~\ref{sec:inputs}).
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item $C_M \geq 0$, scalar: paid once per train per depot day ($x_{i0k} = 1$),
        whatever the number of interventions on that day. An idle day costs nothing.
  \item $C_{R,i\ell} \geq 0$: per unit of mean degradation removed by a repair
        (Section~\ref{sec:repair_cost}).
  \item $C_{\mathrm{rep},i\ell} \geq 0$: per replacement; required, no default.
  \item $C_D \geq 0$: weight of $u$ (Section~\ref{sec:damage_reg}); the term is added
        once in every mode.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints}
\label{sec:constraints}
% ================================================================

Every row holds for every day $k = 1, \ldots, T$ unless its section says otherwise.

\subsection{Assignment Constraints}
\label{sec:assignment}

Each train performs at most one activity per day:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j=0}^{M} x_{ijk} \leq 1 \qquad \forall\, i, k .
  \label{eq:a_one_activity}
\end{equation}
Each mission is flown by exactly one train every day:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i=1}^{F} x_{ijk} = 1 \qquad \forall\, j \in \{1, \ldots, M\},\; k .
  \label{eq:a_coverage}
\end{equation}
Exactly $M$ trains fly every day; the other $F - M$ trains are in the depot or idle.
No other fleet-availability row is built. An idle day is an all-zero assignment row.
Depot days and idle days add no increment.

\subsection{Depot-Day Compatibility}
\label{sec:depot}

A component may be repaired or replaced only on a depot day of its train, and at most
one of the two on the same day:
\begin{equation}
  x^m_{i\ell k} + x^r_{i\ell k} \leq x_{i0k} \qquad \forall\, i, \ell, k .
  \label{eq:a_depot}
\end{equation}
With \texttt{allow\_replacement = false} the row reads $x^m_{i\ell k} \leq x_{i0k}$.
Several components of one train may receive interventions on the same depot day; a
component without an intervention keeps its state.

\subsection{Repair Laws}
\label{sec:repair_laws}

The input \texttt{repair\_model} selects the law per cell (default \texttt{ardinf});
the repair efficiency is $\rho = \rho_{i\ell} \in (0, 1]$. With $D^-$ the degradation
before a repair on day $k$ (end of day $k-1$) and $D^+$ the degradation after it:
\begin{align}
  \ARDinf\ (\texttt{ardinf}):&\quad D^+ = (1-\rho)\, D^- ,
    \label{eq:a_ardinf} \\
  \ARDone\ (\texttt{ard1}):&\quad D^+ = D^- - \rho\,\bigl(D^- - D^{\mathrm{last}}\bigr),
    \label{eq:a_ard1}
\end{align}
with $D^{\mathrm{last}}$ the level just after the last intervention (repair or
replacement); before the first intervention of the horizon, the level just after the
last intervention before it. Every model accepts both laws.

\textbf{Repair map.} For a tracked quantity $s$ with anchor $g$, a repair on day $k$
gives
\begin{equation}
  \psi(s_{k-1}, g_{k-1}) =
  \begin{cases}
    a\, s_{k-1} & \ARDinf, \\
    a\, s_{k-1} + (1-a)\, g_{k-1} & \ARDone,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:a_psi}
\end{equation}
with the repair factor $a = 1 - \rho$ for a mean and for $K$, $a = (1-\rho)^2$ for a
variance and for $R$, and $a = 1$ for the Wiener variance (both laws). The model
sections give the factor of each tracked quantity.

\textbf{Anchors} ($\ARDone$ only; one per tracked quantity, none for the Wiener
variance):
\begin{equation}
  g_0 = \text{starting anchor}, \qquad
  g_k = \begin{cases}
    s_k & \text{on a repair or replacement day}, \\
    g_{k-1} & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:a_anchor}
\end{equation}
The starting anchors $g^{\mu}_{i\ell 0}$, $g^{v}_{i\ell 0}$, $g^{R}_{i\ell 0}$,
$g^{K}_{i\ell 0}$ are the optional inputs \texttt{anchor\_mu\_0}, \texttt{anchor\_v\_0},
\texttt{anchor\_R\_0}, \texttt{anchor\_K\_0}; the default of each is the initial value
of its quantity; each satisfies $0 \leq g_0 \leq s_0$
(Section~\ref{sec:consistency_checks}).

\subsection{Encoding of the Dynamics}
\label{sec:encoding}

Every dynamics row is one-sided ($\geq$); replacement rows and anchor rows are
equalities. For one tracked quantity $s$ of a cell (a mean, a variance, $R$ or $K$):
day increment $\delta s_k$, natural range $M_s$, replacement value $s^{\new}$, anchor
$g$ ($\ARDone$ only), repair map $\psi$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:a_psi}), cell binaries $x^m_k$,
$x^r_k$, and $e_k = x^m_k + x^r_k$. The input \texttt{encoding} selects the form per
cell.

\emph{Big-M form} (\texttt{encoding = "bigm"}, the default):
\begin{align}
  &\text{no intervention:} & s_k &\geq s_{k-1} + \delta s_k - M_s\,(x^m_k + x^r_k),
    \label{eq:a_bigm_carry} \\
  &\text{repair:} & s_k &\geq \psi(s_{k-1}, g_{k-1}) - M_s\,(1 - x^m_k),
    \label{eq:a_bigm_repair} \\
  &\text{replacement:} & |s_k - s^{\new}| &\leq M_s\,(1 - x^r_k),
    \label{eq:a_bigm_repl} \\
  &\text{anchor } (\ARDone)\text{:} & |g_k - s_k| &\leq M_s\,(1 - e_k), \qquad
    |g_k - g_{k-1}| \leq M_s\, e_k .
    \label{eq:a_bigm_anchor}
\end{align}
Each row with an absolute value is built as two linear rows.

\emph{Indicator form} (\texttt{encoding = "indicator"}). One binary $n_{i\ell k}$ per
cell and day, and Gurobi indicator constraints:
\begin{equation}
  n_{i\ell k} + x^m_{i\ell k} + x^r_{i\ell k} = 1 ,
  \label{eq:a_ind_sum}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
  n_k = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\; s_k \geq s_{k-1} + \delta s_k, &
  x^m_k = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\; s_k \geq \psi(s_{k-1}, g_{k-1}), &
  x^r_k = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\; s_k = s^{\new}, \label{eq:a_ind_state} \\
  n_k = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\; g_k = g_{k-1}, &
  x^m_k = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\; g_k = s_k, &
  x^r_k = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\; g_k = s_k. \label{eq:a_ind_anchor}
\end{align}

\emph{Rules for both forms.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Anchor rows only under $\ARDone$, for every tracked quantity except the Wiener
        variance.
  \item Wiener variance: $\psi = s_{k-1}$, no anchor; it is never left free below on
        a repair day. In the big-M form its no-intervention and repair rows are
        replaced by the single row Eq.~\eqref{eq:w_vrow}.
  \item \texttt{allow\_replacement = false}: no $x^r$; read $x^r_k = 0$ and drop the
        replacement rows.
  \item At binary points the two forms have the same feasible set.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Natural ranges} $M_s$ (big-M constants): $\tau_{i\ell}$ for a mean;
$V_{\max}$ for a Gaussian or Wiener variance; $V^{\mathrm{C}}$ or $V^{\mathrm{B}}$ for
a rainflow variance (Cantelli or Bernstein); $2\tau^2/\kappa$ for $R$;
$s_{i\ell}\tau - \kappa$ for $K$ (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_rainflow}). An anchor has the
range of its quantity.

\subsection{Replacement}
\label{sec:replacement}

The input \texttt{allow\_replacement} (default \texttt{true}) makes replacements
available; when \texttt{false}, the binaries $x^r_{i\ell k}$, the replacement rows and
the replacement cost are not built. On a replacement day every tracked quantity of
the cell takes its replacement value, and every anchor the same value:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item $\mu^{\new}_{i\ell}$: input \texttt{replacement\_mu}, default $0$;
  \item $v^{\new}_{i\ell}$: input \texttt{replacement\_v}, default $0$, where a
        variance is tracked;
  \item $R^{\new}_{i\ell}$, $K^{\new}_{i\ell}$: rainflow Hoeffding and Chernoff,
        Section~\ref{sec:dyn_rainflow}.
\end{itemize}
The rows are Eq.~\eqref{eq:a_bigm_repl} or Eq.~\eqref{eq:a_ind_state}. Each
replacement value lies in the natural range of its quantity, and the replacement
state satisfies every row of a replacement day in the form built at the cell
(Section~\ref{sec:consistency_checks}).

The input \texttt{replacement\_as\_new} (default \texttt{false}) sets every replacement
value of the cell to $0$; a non-zero replacement input together with it is an input
error. A replacement costs $C_{\mathrm{rep},i\ell}$ only ($z_{i\ell k} = 0$ on a
replacement day). A cell may be replaced on any depot day, any number of times.

\subsection{Linear Forms of the Reliability Rows}
\label{sec:linear_forms}

The Gaussian, Wiener, Cantelli, Hoeffding and Bernstein rows have the form
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{i\ell k} \;\leq\; f(\mu_{i\ell k}),
  \qquad f(\mu) = c_2\,(\tau - \mu)^2 + c_1\,(\tau - \mu),
  \qquad \mu_{i\ell k} \leq \bar\mu_{\mathrm{side}},
  \label{eq:a_quad}
\end{equation}
with $f'(\mu) = -2c_2(\tau - \mu) - c_1$; the third relation is the side row.

\begingroup\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}lllll@{}}
\toprule
Row & $\chi$ & $c_2$ & $c_1$ & $\bar\mu_{\mathrm{side}}$ \\
\midrule
Gaussian, Wiener & $v$ & $1/\kappa_G^2$ & $0$ & $\tau$ \\
Rainflow, Cantelli & $v$ & $1/\kappa_C^2 = \varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)$ & $0$ & $\tau$ \\
Rainflow, Hoeffding & $R$ & $2/\kappa$ & $0$ & $\tau$ \\
Rainflow, Bernstein & $v$ & $1/(2\kappa)$ & $-b_{i\ell}/3$ & $\tau - c_{i\ell}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\endgroup

The input \texttt{reliability\_impl} selects the form per cell; every form keeps the
side row.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \texttt{exact}: Eq.~\eqref{eq:a_quad}, a nonconvex quadratic constraint.
        Gurobi's \texttt{NonConvex} is set to $2$ when at least one cell has a
        quadratic row in this form.
  \item \texttt{tangent}: one linear row per day, at
        $\mu_r = \texttt{tangent\_ref}_{i\ell}\cdot\tau_{i\ell}$ (\texttt{tangent\_ref}
        per cell, in $[0, 1)$, default $0.5$):
        \begin{equation}
          \chi_{i\ell k} \;\leq\; f(\mu_r) + f'(\mu_r)\,(\mu_{i\ell k} - \mu_r).
          \label{eq:a_tangent}
        \end{equation}
        At a Bernstein cell, $\mu_r < \sqrt{\tau\,(\tau - 2c_{i\ell})}$
        (Section~\ref{sec:consistency_checks}).
  \item \texttt{pwl}: $P = \texttt{pwl\_points}$ equal segments
        $[(p-1)\tau/P,\; p\tau/P]$ of $[0, \tau]$ (default $P = 8$), midpoints
        $\hat\mu_p = (p - \tfrac12)\tau/P$, and one binary $w_{i\ell kp}$ per cell,
        day and segment:
        \begin{align}
          \textstyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} w_{i\ell kp} &= 1, \label{eq:a_pwl_sum} \\
          w_{i\ell kp} = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\;
            (p-1)\,\tau/P \leq \mu_{i\ell k} \leq p\,\tau/P, \label{eq:a_pwl_seg} \\
          w_{i\ell kp} = 1 &\;\Rightarrow\;
            \chi_{i\ell k} \leq f(\hat\mu_p) + f'(\hat\mu_p)\,(\mu_{i\ell k} - \hat\mu_p).
            \label{eq:a_pwl_cap}
        \end{align}
        The implications are Gurobi indicator constraints in both encodings. With
        $P = 1$: the single tangent at $\tau/2$, no binaries. At a Bernstein cell,
        $\tau/(2P) < \sqrt{\tau\,(\tau - 2c_{i\ell})}$
        (Section~\ref{sec:consistency_checks}).
\end{itemize}
At a cell whose reliability row is linear (Markov, Chernoff, inverse Gaussian, Gamma)
the row is built in its exact form and the value of \texttt{reliability\_impl} is
ignored. An unknown value is an input error at every cell. Each linear form implies
the exact row (scoping document, section ``Linear forms of the reliability rows'').

\subsection{Repair Cost Variable}
\label{sec:repair_cost}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

$z_{i\ell k} \geq 0$ is the mean degradation removed by a repair of cell $(i,\ell)$ on
day $k$, for every model and both laws. It is built at cells with
$C_{R,i\ell} > 0$, on every day $k = 1, \ldots, T$ except the transitory days in the
mode \texttt{operating\_average}. Only the lower rows are built:
\begin{equation}
  z_{i\ell k} \;\geq\; \rho_{i\ell}\,\bigl(\mu_{i\ell,k-1} - g^{\mu}_{i\ell,k-1}\bigr)
    - \rho_{i\ell}\,\tau_{i\ell}\,\bigl(1 - x^m_{i\ell k}\bigr),
  \qquad z_{i\ell k} \geq 0,
  \label{eq:a_z}
\end{equation}
with $g^{\mu} \equiv 0$ under $\ARDinf$. At the optimum $z_{i\ell k} = 0$ on every day
without a repair, replacement days included. $z$ is an upper bound on the expected
removed degradation, except at the first repair when the starting anchor is above the
true one (scoping document, section ``Cost function'').

\subsection{Damage Penalty}
\label{sec:damage_reg}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

The scalar $u \geq 0$ and its rows are built only when $C_D > 0$; with $C_D = 0$ the
input \texttt{penalty\_type} is ignored. One row per train and day:
\begin{align}
  \texttt{inf\_norm} \text{ (default)}:&\quad
    u \;\geq\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \mu_{i\ell k} \qquad \forall\, i, k,
    \label{eq:a_u_inf} \\
  \texttt{quadratic}:&\quad
    u \;\geq\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \mu_{i\ell k}^2 \qquad \forall\, i, k.
    \label{eq:a_u_quad}
\end{align}
At the optimum $u = \max_{i,k} \sum_{\ell} \mu_{i\ell k}$ (\texttt{inf\_norm}) or
$u = \max_{i,k} \sum_{\ell} \mu_{i\ell k}^2$ (\texttt{quadratic}): the largest damage of
one train on one day. Eq.~\eqref{eq:a_u_quad} is a rotated second-order cone; it does
not need \texttt{NonConvex}. If $C_D > 0$ and \texttt{penalty\_type = "quadratic"}, every cell
with a quadratic reliability row must have \texttt{reliability\_impl = "exact"};
otherwise a \texttt{ValueError} is raised (Section~\ref{sec:consistency_checks}). At a
cell with a linear row the value is ignored. With $C_D = 0$ the rule does not apply.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Loop Constraint}
\label{sec:loop}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------

One hard row per cell and per coordinate $s$ of its tracked state:
\begin{equation}
  s_{i\ell T} \;\leq\; s_{i\ell H_1}.
  \label{eq:a_loop}
\end{equation}
The rows cover every tracked quantity of the cell (the mean, and the variance, $R$ or
$K$ where tracked) and, under $\ARDone$, every anchor. Each coordinate has its own
row; no row compares a scalar function of them, such as a failure-probability bound.
There is no soft loop penalty. With these rows every day of every repetition of the
operating phase satisfies the reliability requirement, for every model, both laws,
both encodings, every form, and any $H_1$, $H_2$ (scoping document, section
``Stochastic loop constraint'').

\emph{Consequences.} An $\ARDone$ cell with $\rho_{i\ell} < 1$ whose train flies, in the
operating phase, a mission with a positive mean increment for the cell must be
replaced at least once in that phase; with \texttt{allow\_replacement = false} its
train cannot fly such a mission there, and the model is infeasible if the fleet
cannot cover the missions otherwise (scoping document, section ``Stochastic loop
constraint''). A Wiener cell likewise needs a replacement in the operating phase
when its train flies a mission there (Section~\ref{sec:dyn_wiener}).
